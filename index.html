Top 5 0DTE OTM Option Tips

Same-day-expiry out-of-the-money options are very high risk. Numbers below are general rules of thumb, not statistics or guarantees. Diagrams are illustrative only.

1. Size it like a lottery ticket

0DTE OTM options can go to $0 in minutes. Keep each trade tiny — a common rule of thumb is ≤1% of the account — so a full loss is routine, not damaging.

📏 Rule of thumb: risk ≈ 0.5–1% per 0DTE trade; never add to a loser.

2. Respect theta — timing is everything

With no days left, the option is almost all time value, and it bleeds fast. The first hour after the open and the last hour usually have the strongest moves; midday chop tends to drain premium.

📏 Rule of thumb: don't sit in a flat 0DTE position through midday chop.

3. Pick near-the-money, liquid strikes

Far-OTM strikes are cheap because they rarely pay. Strikes a little OTM (delta roughly 0.15–0.30) need a smaller move to work. Stick to liquid underlyings (SPY, QQQ, SPX) with tight bid/ask spreads and use limit orders.

📏 Rule of thumb: delta ~0.15–0.30; skip if the spread is a big % of the price.

4. Take profits fast with a ladder

Gains on 0DTE options vanish as fast as they appear. Sell into strength with a plan (for example, half at +100%, then scale out), and set a hard time stop or premium stop (such as −50%) before you enter.

📏 Rule of thumb: sell half at +100%; exit if the thesis hasn't worked within your time window.

5. Trade a level or catalyst, not hope

Enter only when there's a reason: a key level reclaim after a liquidity sweep, a break-and-retest, or a scheduled catalyst (CPI, FOMC). Define the stop on the underlying's chart — if price loses the level, you're out.

📏 Rule of thumb: no setup, no trade; your stop is the level, not a feeling.

Pattern Recap

Quick-reference reminder: top 5 order-flow and top 5 ICT concepts. Confirm with context — no pattern works alone.

Order Flow

Absorption

What: Heavy aggressive market orders hit a level but passive limit orders soak them up, so price doesn't move.

Spot: High volume / large delta at one price with little or no price progress; repeated tests of the level hold.

▲ Sellers absorbed at a low (big sell volume, price holds) → longs.

▼ Buyers absorbed at a high (big buy volume, price stalls) → shorts.

Exhaustion

What: The aggressive side runs out of participants at the end of a move — volume dries up at the extreme.

Spot: Thinning volume/delta at the tip of a push (small prints at the high/low of the footprint bar), often with a wick.

▲ Selling dries up at new lows → reversal up.

▼ Buying dries up at new highs → reversal down.

Stacked imbalances

What: Diagonal bid-vs-ask imbalance (typically ≥300%) at 3+ consecutive price levels in a footprint bar.

Spot: Footprint shows a vertical stack of highlighted imbalance cells; the zone often acts as support/resistance later.

▲ Stacked ask (buy) imbalances → aggressive buyers; zone = support.

▼ Stacked bid (sell) imbalances → aggressive sellers; zone = resistance.

Delta divergence

What: Price makes a new high/low but cumulative delta (buy − sell aggression) does not confirm it.

Spot: Compare swing points on price vs. cumulative delta (CVD); non-confirmation = weakening aggression.

▲ Price lower low, delta higher low → selling weakening.

▼ Price higher high, delta lower high → buying weakening.

Iceberg / large-lot initiative

What: Hidden large limit order that keeps refilling (iceberg), or big block trades initiating a move.

Spot: Traded volume at a price far exceeds displayed DOM size and the level reloads; or big-lot prints lead a breakout.

▲ Bid iceberg holds / large buy prints lift offers → longs.

▼ Offer iceberg caps / large sell prints hit bids → shorts.

ICT (Inner Circle Trader)

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

What: 3-candle imbalance: candle 1's high and candle 3's low don't overlap, leaving an inefficient gap.

Spot: Big displacement candle; the gap between candle 1 wick and candle 3 wick. Price often returns to fill it.

▲ Bullish FVG (gap below price) → buy on retrace into it.

▼ Bearish FVG (gap above price) → sell on retrace into it.

Order Block (OB)

What: Last opposite-colored candle before a strong displacement move that breaks structure.

Spot: Find the last down candle before a sharp rally (or up candle before a drop); mark its body/range as the zone.

▲ Bullish OB = last down candle before rally → buy the retest.

▼ Bearish OB = last up candle before drop → sell the retest.

Liquidity sweep / stop hunt

What: Price runs above old highs / below old lows to trigger resting stops, then reverses.

Spot: Equal highs/lows or obvious swing points get wicked through, then price closes back inside the range.

▲ Sweep of sell-side liquidity (lows) then reclaim → long.

▼ Sweep of buy-side liquidity (highs) then rejection → short.

Market Structure Shift / BOS

What: BOS = break of a swing point in the trend's direction (continuation); MSS/CHoCH = first break against it (reversal).

Spot: Track swing highs/lows; a close beyond the last opposing swing with displacement marks the shift.

▲ Downtrend breaks above last lower high → bullish shift.

▼ Uptrend breaks below last higher low → bearish shift.

OTE / Premium–Discount

What: Fibonacci of a dealing range: above 50% = premium, below = discount; OTE = 62–79% retracement (~70.5% sweet spot).

Spot: Draw fib from swing low to high (or high to low) of the impulse; wait for retrace into the 0.62–0.79 band.

▲ Buy in discount / bullish OTE of an up-leg.

▼ Sell in premium / bearish OTE of a down-leg.
